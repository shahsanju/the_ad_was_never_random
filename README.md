# The Ad Was Never Random: How Streaming Platforms Know the Worst Possible Moment to Interrupt You

A technical exploration of ad placement, AI, streaming infrastructure, and viewer psychology.

**Author: Sanjana Shah**

---

## Introduction

This article began with a simple question:

*Why do Prime Video ads always seem to appear at the exact moment when I am most invested in the story?*

What started as a small moment of frustration turned into a much deeper investigation into how modern streaming platforms deliver advertisements. Along the way, I explored server-side ad insertion (SSAI), SCTE-35 markers, AI-based content analysis, viewer behavior signals, real-time ad auctions, and the business decisions that shape the streaming experience.

The deeper I looked, the more I realized that an advertisement appearing at the "worst possible moment" might not be a coincidence at all.

## Technical Note

The infrastructure discussed in this article — including server-side ad insertion (SSAI), SCTE-35 markers, AI-assisted content analysis, personalized streaming, and real-time ad auctions — is based on publicly available documentation and common industry practices.

The strategic theories discussed later, including the "hook-and-harvest" pattern and the idea that certain interruption points may intentionally encourage viewers to return for another session, are my personal hypotheses based on observation, behavioral psychology, and engineering reasoning. They are not publicly confirmed strategies used by Amazon.
